\begin{abstract}
Purely functional programming is attractive for its equational reasoning, but
without in-place updates some operations become asymptotically more expensive:
an array write that copies under sustained sharing can turn a linear traversal
into a quadratic one. Lean~4 recovers in-place updates through a \emph{dynamic}
mechanism: the runtime updates an array in place exactly when its reference count
is one, and copies otherwise. The branch is decided at run time, by the heap's
current sharing, not by the program's type. This paper asks whether a static type
discipline can arrange that the dynamic check succeeds by construction at every
update it certifies. Linear and affine type systems answer this question for
their host languages by changing the type system---linear arrows, uniqueness or
ownership types, or a borrow checker. We present a light-weight alternative for
Lean that leaves the core type system untouched: a checked ownership discipline
over a fragment of Lean's compiler IR, with a model heap and counts, a decidable
checker whose soundness is proved in Lean, strict recognisers for the emitted
array-write prefixes, and a correspondence between the recognised LCNF and the
model. We derive the temporary extraction pair's count accounting and the
put-back's exclusive branch, build a concrete runtime model in which the
exclusivity check cannot abort under the correspondence, and bind the result to
the pinned toolchain with an artifact/IR/C gate and a differential corpus. The
guarantee carries two accounting contracts---the environment owner count and the
heap/runtime count fidelity---and a kernel countermodel shows that the raw
invariant does not imply them. The boundary is explicit: the C runtime's
semantics, replay-to-backend identity, the emitted recursive callee, the counted
protocol's nested execution composition, the extraction/restoration event
coverage and the source correspondence remain assumptions or open obligations.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Reference-counted functional languages such as Lean~4 and Koka update a shared
array by copying it and leave the in-place branch to the runtime's exclusivity
test \cite{lean4,perceus,countingbeans}. The resulting cost is data-dependent and
invisible in the type: the same source program can copy or not depending on how
many references happen to exist at the write. A static discipline that guarantees
the count is one at the write would make the in-place branch a property of the
program rather than of its current heap, and would let a library or a compiler
rule out the copy branch without a dynamic test.

Lean~4 has no linear, affine or uniqueness types, and this development does not
add them. The distinction is not the absence of a kernel change but the verified
composition: a count-one checker, the recognised emitted prefix, the
extraction/restoration accounting and a runtime model, joined at the boundary
the paper names. Linearity is a checked property of a recognised fragment under
named contracts, not a typing judgement for every program.
\subsection{The chain to an in-place write}\label{sec:chain}
The result is a chain of eight conditional steps. Read in order, they say why an
accepted update is in place; each names the premise it uses and the control that
shows the premise is necessary, and the sections that follow prove it.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.8em, itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{The entry state.} \lean{EntryInv} gives unique ownership, client
separation and root completeness, and \lean{initEntryInv} establishes it for a
fresh array. Premise: no client alias. Control: the alias context of
Example~2 is refused.
\item \textbf{Static acceptance.} A write requires an owned handle of
multiplicity one; \lean{check\_sound} and \lean{checkN\_sound\_of\_entryInv}
turn acceptance plus \lean{Inv} into an exclusive write in the model. Control:
the held context with multiplicity two is refused.
\item \textbf{Recognition.} \lean{execAST\_recognized} and its nested
counterpart make the recognition hypothesis load-bearing: the executed trace is
the recognised prefix. Control: the token-preserving near misses in the mutation corpus are rejected.
\item \textbf{Correspondence.} The three theorems
\lean{project\_sound},
\lean{recognized\_write\_in\_place} and
\lean{index\_path\_simulates\_model}
identify the emitted outcome with the model's \lean{inPlaceOutcome}. Premise:
the layout, agreement and entry facts, the finite-path \lean{PathContract}, and
the contracts A1--A12/N1--N12.
\item \textbf{Temporary handles.} \lean{pair\_accounting} shows the extraction
pair returns the parent and child counts to their entry values; the no-free and
retained-alias controls leave a count of two.
\item \textbf{Restoration.} \lean{putBack\_exclusive\_of\_wellFormed} derives the
mutate branch from the counted boundary and the child-call boundary; the shared,
persistent and retained-parent controls reach the copy branch or are excluded.
\item \textbf{Runtime model.} Under \lean{Corr}, \lean{checkExclusive\_no\_abort}
and \lean{setAt\_inPlace\_of\_entryInv} make the check return and the set mutate;
\lean{prefix\_abort\_impossible} states the same at the emitted-semantics
boundary. Controls: shared and persistent sources, and a dirty allocator cursor.
\item \textbf{Linked corpus.} The gate and the differential corpus observe the
agreement of the primitives on the pinned toolchain; the closed-pure-array case
is the negative control that shows the compiler can still copy outside the
model.
\end{enumerate}

The physical reading also carries the environment-side \lean{OwnersAccounted}
and the heap-side \lean{CountFidelity}; \lean{alias\_not\_accounted} shows why
the raw invariant does not imply the first. The extraction/restoration steps are
the nested counted protocol; the flat \lean{principal\_write} is the composed
flat result, and the nested counted execution composition is outstanding. The
chain is conditional: the C
semantics of the primitives, replay-to-backend identity, address identity, free
and cascade, the emitted callee, the accounting contracts and the source
correspondence are not part of it and are listed in \Cref{sec:limits}. The
controls are not footnotes: without each premise the corresponding step fails,
which is why they are proved beside the positive results.

\paragraph{Closest prior work.}
Marc Huisinga's 2023 thesis \cite{huisinga} implements a static uniqueness
analysis for a model of Lean's IR and studies its checker; soundness and compiler
integration remain future work there. Lean's \lean{Array.markLinear}
\cite{marklinear} is a dynamic facility: it can copy an array to establish
uniqueness and, with \lean{LEAN\_ABORT\_ON\_NONLINEAR}, abort on later
nonlinear use. Translation validation \cite{translationvalidation} checks a
particular compiler run; this paper does not validate arbitrary transformations.
The advance here is the verified composition of the count-one checker, the
recognised emitted prefix, the extraction/restoration accounting and a runtime
model, not a claim to the first static uniqueness analysis for Lean.

The paper makes six contributions. (i) A model heap and a decidable checker
whose soundness is a Lean theorem (\Cref{thm:check-sound}); an entry invariant
supplies the heap facts the checker cannot see (\Cref{thm:entry-inv}).
(ii) Strict recognisers for the emitted array-write prefixes, with structural
rejection of token-preserving near misses. (iii) A correspondence from the
recognised LCNF to the model (\Cref{thm:prefix-sound} and \Cref{thm:path-sound}), including
the finite nested path. (iv) A counted extraction/call/restoration argument
(\Cref{thm:pair} and \Cref{thm:putback}). (v) A concrete runtime model and an
abort-impossibility theorem (\Cref{thm:no-abort}), bound to the pinned toolchain
by a gate and a differential corpus. (vi) The environment-side and heap-side
accounting contracts (\lean{OwnersAccounted}, \lean{CountFidelity}), with a
kernel countermodel showing why the raw invariant does not imply them, and the
composed flat theorem \lean{principal\_write}.

The discipline is a deep embedding: \lean{Code} and \lean{NCode} are explicit
syntax interpreted by \lean{run}/\lean{runN}, and \lean{Ctx}, \lean{Heap},
\lean{check} and the theorems are ordinary Lean definitions. The source is Lean,
lowered to LCNF; a recognised prefix is mapped to a model program. The guarantee
is conditional on the named contracts, including the environment-side accounting
and the heap/runtime count fidelity, and it does not extend to the linked C
runtime beyond the measured gate.

\section{Setting: Lean 4, LCNF, and reference counting}
Lean~4 compiles through a sequence of IRs. The last LCNF phase inserts
reference-count operations, and the backend emits C. The runtime decides an array
update with \lean{lean\_is\_exclusive}: count one and non-persistent means in
place; every other case copies. The runtime is single-threaded; count one and
non-persistence characterise this test in that setting and are not a claim about
every Lean runtime mode. The development pins the toolchain, the sibling
revision and the captured LCNF artifacts, so every claim names the code it is
about.
The problem is not to implement reference counting, but to know statically that
the count at a particular write is one. Lean's source type system has no
linearity or uniqueness; adding one would be a language change. We therefore
state the property over the compiler's code and prove the connection, rather
than extend the type system.

\subsection{Model programs}
The checker does not run on Lean source or on LCNF directly. Its programs are
two small inductive types, interpreted over the model heap and environment; the
compiler connection is a mapping from the recognised LCNF to these values.

\begin{minted}{lean4}
inductive LetF where
  | lit (n : Nat)
  | size (a : Var)
  | isShared (x : Var)
  | checkAlias (x : Var)

inductive Code where
  | let (x : Var) (v : LetF) (k : Code)
  | inc (x : Var) (n : Nat) (k : Code)
  | dec (x : Var) (n : Nat) (k : Code)
  | check (x : Var) (k : Code)
  | setArr (x : Var) (i : Var) (v : Var) (k : Code)
  | ret (x : Var)

inductive NCode where
  | base (c : Code)
  | takeOut (a i fst snd : Var) (e : Ref) (k : NCode)
  | putBack (a e i result : Var) (k : NCode)
  | callAt (state path x result : Var) (body : NCode) (k : NCode)
  | ret (x : Var)
\end{minted}

A \lean{Code} is the straight-line array-write fragment; an \lean{NCode} adds
extraction, restoration and the recursive call. Variables are \lean{Nat},
references are \lean{Nat}, \lean{Val} is a reference, a scalar or erased, and a
\lean{Heap} maps references to objects with a field array, a count and a
persistence flag. \lean{run}/\lean{runN} execute these values and
\lean{check}/\lean{checkN} are the checkers; the soundness theorems are about
this semantics. They are neither Lean source nor LCNF: \lean{projectPrefix} and
\lean{projectNested} walk an LCNF value, \lean{Prefix.toModel} and
\lean{indexModelTerm} read a \lean{Code}/\lean{NCode} off the recognised
operands, and \lean{execAST}/\lean{execNested} execute the recognised trace. The
correspondence theorems relate that execution to the model run.

\subsection{A running example: the nested write}
The source is the array case of \lean{Layout5.setAt} in the sibling. It takes
the element out, checks the extracted focus, recurses on it and puts the result
back:
\begin{minted}{lean4}
| _, .index i q, y, s =>
    if h : i < s.size then
      match takeOutAt (assertExclusiveArray openSite s) i h (stateDefault _) with
      | ⟨(sib, focus), hbound⟩ =>
        plugAt sib i hbound (setAt q y (assertExclusive structSite focus))
    else s
\end{minted}
The emitted C for this arm is the sequence the development names: a
\lean{lean\_is\_exclusive} test for the array, the \lean{takeOut} exchange
(\lean{lean\_array\_fget}, \lean{lean\_array\_fset}, the pair allocation), the
child check on the extracted element, the recursive call and the
\lean{lean\_array\_fset} put-back. The recogniser \lean{projectNested} accepts
the \lean{Path.index} arm, \lean{PairProjection} accepts the two pair arms, and
\lean{execNestedAST} executes the accepted trace. The model term is
\lean{Path.build path value body ls}; \lean{checkN} checks it from
\lean{initCtx}.

The example separates what the tool computes from what a client supplies. The
compiler computes the LCNF and the C; \lean{projectNested} computes the prefix;
\lean{Prefix.toModel}/\lean{indexModelTerm} compute the model program;
\lean{checkN} computes the checker result. The client supplies the source
declaration, the \lean{Path} and the take-out name, and the proofs of
\lean{Agrees}, \lean{LevelMatch}, \lean{PathContract} and \lean{EntryInv} (the
path/slot and entry facts the checker cannot see). The finite-path induction
discharges the tail contract; the leaf contract and the emitted call's meaning
(N10) remain. A nearby rejected variant is the flat \lean{aliasSet} example
below, refused because the array's multiplicity is two.

\section{The ownership discipline}

\subsection{The judgement and its rules}
A context records, for each variable, whether it is owned, borrowed, scalar or
dead, which reference it denotes, and the multiplicity of that reference. The
checker is a total function from a context and a program to a successor context
or failure; the frame stack records open extractions. The rules in
Table~\ref{tab:rules} are ownership obligations: a write is admitted only for an
owned handle of multiplicity one, an extraction only with the frame discipline,
and a call only with an isolated body that returns the state it was handed.

The object language is untyped: there is no type former for a program, and the
checker does not infer one by unification. The typing judgement is
$c \vdash \mathit{code} \Rightarrow c'$, defined to hold exactly when
\lean{check c code = some c'}; the context is the type of the program point. A
context is a usage map, a reference map and a multiplicity map, and the checker
is the syntax-directed decision procedure for the rules below.

\begin{minted}{lean4}
inductive U where
  | owned
  | borrow
  | scalar
  | dead

structure Ctx where
  us    : Var → U
  refOf : Var → Option Ref
  mult  : Ref → Nat

structure Frame where
  arr  : Ref
  idx  : Var
  elem : Ref

def check   : Ctx → Code → Option Ctx
def checkNF : Ctx → List Frame → NCode → Option Ctx
\end{minted}

The judgement is syntax-directed: each constructor has one rule, the context is
an input, and the successor context is computed by \lean{setUs}, \lean{move},
\lean{bindOwned}, \lean{inc} and \lean{dec}. There are no metavariables, no
unification and no implicit type arguments in the object language; the only
computation is the context update. This is why the discipline makes the count
visible at the type level: at the write, the array's handle has usage
\lean{owned} and multiplicity one in the context, and the rule refuses every
other usage.

\centering
\small
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{3.3cm}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{6.4cm}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}X@{}}

\midrule
\lean{let x (lit n)} & --- & \lean{x} scalar \\

Two consequences of the table are worth stating. First, \lean{dec} is affine,
not the last drop: its premise is $n <$ the object's multiplicity, so it
consumes a duplicate handle and leaves the object live. The last release would
free the cell, which this fragment does not model; free and cascade are named
limits. Second, linearity at the write is the side condition
\lean{multOf x = 1}, not the usage tag alone: \lean{us x = owned} says the
variable names the object, while the multiplicity says it is the only handle.

\paragraph{A derivation.}
The first example is the two rules applied in sequence. The premise of the
second rule is the context the first produces; no type variable is solved.
\[
\frac{
  \begin{array}{c}
  \text{us } 0 = \text{owned} \qquad \text{multOf } 0 = 1 \\
  c_1 = c_0.\text{move } 0\ 3
  \end{array}
}{
  c_0 \vdash \texttt{checkAlias } 0 \Rightarrow c_1
}
\qquad
\frac{
  \begin{array}{c}
  \text{us } 3 = \text{owned} \qquad \text{multOf } 3 = 1 \\
  \text{us } 1 = \text{us } 2 = \text{scalar} \qquad
  c_1 \vdash \texttt{ret } 3 \Rightarrow c_1
  \end{array}
}{
  c_1 \vdash \texttt{setArr } 3\ 1\ 2;\ \texttt{ret } 3 \Rightarrow c_1
}
\]
Composing the two gives
$c_0 \vdash \texttt{execSet } 0\ 3\ 1\ 2 \Rightarrow c_1$, i.e.
\lean{check (initCtx 0) (execSet 0 3 1 2) = some ((initCtx 0).move 0 3)}.
The checker computes $c_1$; the derivation is the rule applications, and the
accepted program is the one the first example's guard checks.

The frame stack is the local form of the ownership invariant. An extraction
opens a frame naming the array, index and element; a put-back closes the matching
frame; a call checks its body under an isolated context and continues with the
result. The exit rule is deliberately conservative: with one open frame only the
extracted element may return, and a deeper stack must be restored first. These
are the rules that make nested updates safe, and they are exactly the parts a
source-level linear type system would encode in types; here they are a checker.

The checker sees the shape of the context, not the heap: slot contents, counts,
client separation and root completeness are supplied by \lean{Inv} and
\lean{EntryInv}, and by the fixture's heap.

\subsection{Worked examples}
Each example is a \lean{Code} or \lean{NCode} value; the \lean{\#guard} is the
executable check, and the surrounding context and heap are the repository's
fixtures. The first two isolate the write rule; the next two the frame stack;
the last two the call's isolation.

\needspace{10\baselineskip}\begin{example}[A unique write]\label{ex:unique}\end{example}
\begin{minted}{lean4}
def execSet (x y i v : Var) : Code :=
  .let y (.checkAlias x) (.setArr y i v (.ret y))

#guard (check (initCtx 0) (execSet 0 3 1 2)).isSome
\end{minted}

\needspace{10\baselineskip}\begin{example}[An aliased write is refused]\label{ex:alias}\end{example}
\begin{minted}{lean4}
def aliasSet (x y i v : Var) : Code :=
  .inc x 1 (execSet x y i v)

#guard (check (initCtx 0) (aliasSet 0 3 1 2)).isNone
\end{minted}
The alias increments the handle, so the write's multiplicity-one obligation
fails; the same refusal appears at the context level for a heap whose count is
two. This is the static counterpart of the runtime copy: the checker rejects the
program before it can copy.

\needspace{10\baselineskip}\begin{example}[An extraction and its return]\label{ex:takeout}\end{example}
\begin{minted}{lean4}
def good : NCode :=
  .takeOut 0 1 2 3 1 (.ret 3)

#guard (checkN ctx0 good).isSome
\end{minted}

\needspace{10\baselineskip}\begin{example}[A stale slot is refused]\label{ex:stale}\end{example}
\begin{minted}{lean4}
def bad : NCode :=
  .takeOut 0 1 2 3 1 (.takeOut 2 1 4 5 1 (.ret 5))

#guard (checkN ctx0 bad).isNone
\end{minted}
The second extraction reopens the array whose slot the first has already
emptied. The frame stack records the first extraction, so the second is refused;
the runtime would fault at that point, which the fixture also checks.

\needspace{10\baselineskip}\begin{example}[A recursive body confined to its state]\label{ex:call}\end{example}
\begin{minted}{lean4}
def good : NCode :=
  .takeOut 0 1 2 3 1
    (.callAt 3 4 7 9 (.ret 3) (.ret 9))

#guard (checkN ctx0 good).isSome
\end{minted}

\needspace{10\baselineskip}\begin{example}[A suspended parent is refused]\label{ex:suspended}\end{example}
\begin{minted}{lean4}
def bad : NCode :=
  .takeOut 0 1 2 3 1
    (.callAt 3 4 7 9
      (.takeOut 2 4 5 6 2 (.putBack 5 6 4 8 (.ret 3)))
      (.ret 9))

#guard (checkN ctx0 bad).isNone
\end{minted}
The refused body returns the call's own state, exactly as the accepted body
does; the difference is isolation. The body runs from an isolated context in
which the suspended parent is dead, so opening it is not a typing error in the
host language but a failure of the checker's ownership obligation.

\subsection{The invariant and soundness}
The model heap, its counts and exclusivity are the semantic side of the
judgement: \lean{Inv} ties the context's multiplicity to the heap count, and
\lean{Exclusive} is count one and non-persistence. The checker's soundness
theorem is the bridge from the static obligation to the heap: every accepted
write selects the exclusive branch. The entry invariant adds the facts a local
checker cannot see: the invariant tree, client separation and root completeness.
For fresh arrays \lean{initEntryInv} establishes it, and
\lean{checkN\_sound\_of\_entryInv} combines it with the checker.

\needspace{10\baselineskip}\begin{claim}[Invariant and exclusivity]\label{def:inv}\end{claim}
\begin{minted}{lean4}
structure Inv (H : Heap) (env : Env) (c : Ctx) : Prop where
  rc_eq         : ∀ r, H.rc r = c.mult r
  owned         : ∀ x r, c.us x = .owned → c.refOf x = some r →
                    env x = some (.ref r) ∧ 1 ≤ c.mult r
  borrow        : ∀ x r, c.us x = .borrow → c.refOf x = some r →
                    env x = some (.ref r) ∧ H.cells r ≠ none
  scalar        : ∀ x, c.us x = .scalar → ∃ n, env x = some (.scalar n)
  nonPersistent : ∀ r o, H.cells r = some o → o.persistent = false
\end{minted}
\begin{minted}{lean4}
def Exclusive (H : Heap) (r : Ref) : Prop :=
  ∃ o, H.cells r = some o ∧ o.rc = 1 ∧ o.persistent = false
\end{minted}

The displayed invariant relates the heap count to the context multiplicity, but it
does not count the environment's owning variables: \lean{Inv.owned} only requires
\lean{1 ≤ c.mult r}. \lean{bindOwned} preserves \lean{Inv} without changing
\lean{mult}, so two owned variables can denote one count-one cell. The raw
invariant therefore does not imply physical uniqueness. The kernel countermodel
\lean{alias\_not\_accounted} constructs exactly that state: \lean{initCtx} has
one owner, and binding a second owned variable leaves the multiplicity at one.

The guarantee therefore carries two accounting contracts. The environment side is
\lean{OwnersAccounted}: multiplicity zero has no owner, and multiplicity one has
at most one owner. The heap side is \lean{CountFidelity}: the object's count is
its incoming heap edges plus the owning environment variables in the supplied
support.

\begin{minted}{lean4}
def OwnersAccounted (c : Ctx) : Prop :=
  (∀ r x, c.mult r = 0 → c.us x = .owned → c.refOf x = some r → False) ∧
  (∀ r, c.mult r = 1 → ∀ x y, c.us x = .owned → c.us y = .owned →
      c.refOf x = some r → c.refOf y = some r → x = y)

def IncomingCount (H : Heap) (r : Ref) : Nat :=
  ((List.range H.next).map fun q =>
    match H.cells q with
    | some o => (o.fields.toList.filter (fun v => v = .ref r)).length
    | none => 0).sum

def EnvOwnerCount (c : Ctx) (vars : List Var) (r : Ref) : Nat :=
  (vars.filter (fun x => c.us x = .owned ∧ c.refOf x = some r)).length

def CountFidelity (H : Heap) (c : Ctx) (vars : List Var) : Prop :=
  ∀ r, H.rc r = IncomingCount H r + EnvOwnerCount c vars r
\end{minted}

\lean{Ownership/Accounting.lean} proves preservation of \lean{OwnersAccounted}
under a non-owning \lean{setUs}, \lean{move} of an owned handle, \lean{inc}, and
\lean{bindOwned}/\lean{dec} with explicit preconditions: no existing owner for
the target of \lean{bindOwned}, and at most one other owner for \lean{dec}. The
checker's take-out and \lean{dec} rules do not yet enforce those preconditions;
the principal theorem carries them as client obligations. The heap-side
\lean{CountFidelity} is an implementation assumption, not a theorem about the C
runtime.

\needspace{10\baselineskip}\begin{claim}[Checker soundness]\label{thm:check-sound}\end{claim}
\begin{minted}{lean4}
theorem check_sound {c : Code} {ctx ctx' : Ctx} {H : Heap} {env : Env}
    (hc : check ctx c = some ctx') (hi : Inv H env ctx) :
    ∃ H' env' v ws,
      run c env H = .done H' env' v ws ∧
      (∀ w ∈ ws, w.2 = true) ∧ Inv H' env' ctx'
\end{minted}

\needspace{10\baselineskip}\begin{claim}[Entry invariant]\label{thm:entry-inv}\end{claim}
\begin{minted}{lean4}
structure EntryInv (H : Heap) (env : Env) (c : Ctx) (root : Ref)
    (client : List Ref) : Prop extends Inv H env c where
  uniqueTree : UniqueTree H root
  separated  : ClientSeparated H root client
  complete   : RootsComplete c H root
\end{minted}
\begin{minted}{lean4}
theorem checkN_sound_of_entryInv
    (hEntry : EntryInv H env c root client)
    (hag : Agrees H env code) (hc : checkN c code = some c') :
    ∃ H' env' v ws,
      runN code env H = .done H' env' v ws ∧
      (∀ w ∈ ws, w.2 = true) ∧ Inv H' env' c'
\end{minted}
\noindent{\small\itshape The quoted signature is the repository's; the heap and environment
parameters are implicit.}

The checker's local rules do not name the heap's path structure. The path theory
supplies it: agreement composes through take-out, call and put-back,
annotation reachability follows the path, and the safety results lift the checker
to every finite path. This is where the per-client proof obligations are
replaced by structural facts.

\section{Recognising the emitted write}
A source-level discipline is only useful if it survives lowering. The compiler
rewrites the program, inlines helpers and chooses the emitted shape; the
recogniser therefore walks the actual LCNF value, pinning operation order,
arities, operand identities and branch constructors. It rejects the token-preserving near misses in its mutation corpus (eleven at the
top level, ten on the nested path, fourteen per pair arm), so the correspondence
is about the code we name, not a look-alike. No universal rejection theorem is
claimed. The accepted grammar fixes the check's argument, the array-set's array,
index and value operands, the instruction order from check to set, the pair
constructor and its two projections, the two \lean{Path} branches and their join,
and the continuation boundary; every other shape is rejected.
Three shapes are recognised: the top-level \lean{execP} write, the nested
\lean{setAt} path, and the two \lean{Path.left}/\lean{Path.right} pair arms with
their joins. The pair arms are the test that the recogniser handles control flow
structurally rather than flattening it; the path and the pair carry their own
contracts (A1--A12 and N1--N12), named where they are assumed.

\section{Correspondence to the ownership model}
The checker is about the model, not about the compiler. The bridge executes the
operation trace the recogniser emitted for the actual declaration and proves it
equal to the model run; the recognition hypothesis is load-bearing because
\lean{execAST} returns nothing unless the code decodes to the expected prefix.
The primitive meanings are the named contracts, not theorems about Lean's
backend.

\noindent{\small\itshape The layout and the heap/environment parameters are implicit, as in the

\noindent{\small\itshape The machine, environment and heap are implicit.

\lean{PrefixOutcome} records the target, the result binder, the heap, the
disposition, the fault and the write trace. It discards the machine's variable map
and any unrecognised suffix. The write trace preserves each covered write's
exclusive/copy bit, so a projection to final contents would lose the property and
is not used. For the flat designated prefix the trace is the single
\lean{(root, true)} event, so the all-recorded-writes predicate is not vacuous
there. The recogniser and executor share the prefix representation;
\lean{execAST\_recognized} is the decoding adequacy lemma, not an independent
instruction semantics. The independent meanings are the primitive contracts
A1--A12; the shared representation is why the decoding hypothesis is
load-bearing.

The nested path is where a finite model meets recursive code. The model nests
take-out, call and put-back per level; the finite-path induction discharges the
callee contract from the tail and leaves only the leaf contract and the emitted
call's meaning (N10). The model's flat environment cannot express the emitted
per-invocation \lean{oproj[1]} index; that bound is recorded rather than hidden.

\section{Counted extraction, call, and restoration}
The emitted take-out creates a pair whose fields are the parent and the
extracted child. The constructor moves the handles into the pair; the
projections increment them; freeing the unique pair releases both edges. The
counts are therefore equal at the boundary, but only because the pair is freed:
the negative controls show that omitting the free, or retaining an alias, leaves
a count of two. Without this accounting, N2's neutrality would be a modeling
premise rather than a derived fact.

\needspace{10\baselineskip}\begin{claim}[Pair accounting]\label{thm:pair}\end{claim}
\begin{minted}{lean4}
theorem pair_accounting {H : Heap} {p c : Ref} {i : Nat}
    (hw : WellFormedEntry H p c i) :
    let B := protocol H p c i
    B.2.rc p = 1 ∧ B.2.rc c = 1 ∧
      B.2.cells p ≠ none ∧ B.2.cells c ≠ none ∧
      B.2.cells B.1 = none ∧
      Heap.Exclusive B.2 p ∧ Heap.Exclusive B.2 c ∧
      B.2.rc p = H.rc p ∧ B.2.rc c = H.rc c
\end{minted}
\begin{minted}{lean4}
theorem no_free_components_not_exclusive
    (hw : WellFormedEntry H p c i) :
    let B := protocolNoFree H p c i
    B.2.rc p = 2 ∧ B.2.rc c = 2 ∧ ¬ Heap.Exclusive B.2 p
\end{minted}

The nested interpreter abstracts the pair increments and the pair release, and it
performs the put-back unconditionally. The dependency closure of

outstanding.

The put-back is the second place where a copy can hide. The operation has two
dispositions: at count one it mutates the parent; at a higher count or a
persistent parent it allocates a fresh cell and decrements the input. The
exclusive branch is derived from the counted-pair boundary and the child-call
boundary, with functional restoration and count preservation separate; the
returned-handle accounting remains a named premise.

\section{Runtime model and abort impossibility}
The model's count is a ghost count; the runtime's is the one that decides. The
runtime model is a separate state machine with its own heap, counts,
persistence, guarded allocator, check and two array-set dispositions. The
structural correspondence preserves refs, cells, counts, persistence, fields and
the cursor; from it the model's exclusivity implies the runtime's, the check
returns, and the write mutates. The abort branch is unreachable under the
correspondence and \lean{EntryInv}.

The definitions are ordinary Lean structures. The correspondence contains no
exclusivity or disposition; those are derived from the preserved counts,
persistence and fields. A guarded allocator refuses a dirty cursor, so the copy
branch cannot overwrite a live cell.

\needspace{10\baselineskip}\begin{claim}[No abort]\label{thm:no-abort}\end{claim}
\begin{minted}{lean4}
theorem checkExclusive_no_abort {H : Heap} {R : RHeap} {r : Ref}
    (hc : Corr H R) (hex : H.Exclusive r) (r' : Ref) :
    checkExclusive R r ≠ Runtime.CheckOutcome.abort r'
\end{minted}

    (hEntry : EntryInv H env (Runner.initCtx root) root client)
    (hC : Corr H R) (hcell : H.cells root = some o)

\section{Limits and non-claims}\label{sec:limits}
\needspace{10\baselineskip}\begin{nonclaim}
The boundary is explicit. The C semantics of the primitives is not formalised;
replay-to-backend identity (A10) and address identity remain assumptions; the
emitted recursive callee and continuation (N10) are not captured; the model's
flat environment cannot express the emitted per-invocation index; the leaf body
contract and several heap-shape facts remain supplied; free/cascade, persistence,
concurrency and callbacks are outside the fragment; and the source
correspondence is not attempted.

The accounting contracts are not consequences of \lean{Inv}. The environment side
(\lean{OwnersAccounted}) has a kernel countermodel and explicit
\lean{bindOwned}/\lean{dec} preconditions that the checker does not yet enforce;
the heap side (\lean{CountFidelity}) is an implementation assumption about the
runtime's count. The recognition claim is scoped to the accepted grammar and the
mutation corpus, not a universal rejection theorem for every token-preserving
near miss. The nested linked runtime is not measured; only the flat probe is. The
nested interpreter abstracts the pair traffic and unconditional restoration, so
the counted protocol's execution composition is outstanding; the nested trace
does not include extraction/restoration stores. The runtime gate's \lean{expect}
checks exit status, and the differential mode compares recorded expectations
rather than executing the Ownership model; the check-enabled modes are the
negative controls.
\end{nonclaim}

\section{Conclusion}
The development proves the count-one discipline at the model, the recognised
LCNF and the runtime model for the flat recognised write, under named contracts,
and composes that guarantee as \lean{principal\_write}. The nested path is a
separate finite model; its counted protocol composition remains open. It measures the linked primitive on a
corpus and gates the pinned evidence. The accounting contracts and the C
semantics, the replay/backend identity, the emitted callee and the source
correspondence remain the boundary. The next step is either a verified runtime
model or a stronger backend gate; the development proves soundness for the
accepted fragment, not completeness of the analysis.
