\begin{abstract}
Reference-counted array updates copy when an extra reference survives. Lean~4
decides the copy at run time, from the heap's count and persistence, so the
in-place branch is not visible in the source type. We mechanise a count-one
ownership discipline for a recognised fragment of Lean's compiler IR and prove,
in Lean~4, (i) that a supported flat array-update fragment, from an accounted
state (\lean{Accounted}) with the store-slot fact supplied (\lean{StoreSlots}),
preserves a complete accounting of owning roots and incoming heap edges at every
state it reaches, (ii) that the emitted extraction pair's count traffic is
neutral at its boundary and that restoration takes the exclusive branch, and
(iii) that the recognised finite path corresponds to the model. The principal
nested theorem composes the finite-path correspondence, the counted
extraction/call/restoration protocol at its accounted boundaries, and the
counted interpreter's recognised-prefix boundary for one arbitrary level of a
finite supported path, from an accounted caller state (\lean{hacc}) and
conditional on a child-call contract (\lean{hcall}), and a closed
kernel-checked instance inhabits it at the recogniser's own layout; the flat
recognised write is the companion result and takes no accounting premise. The
claim stops at that boundary: the emitted recursive callee, trace-level
agreement beyond the counted prefix, extraction/restoration event coverage, C
semantics, replay and address identity, and the source correspondence remain
open, and neither model's count is a physical count.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
Reference-counted array updates copy when the count at the write exceeds one.
Lean~4 decides the branch at run time, from the heap's count and persistence
\cite{lean4,perceus,countingbeans}, so the same source program can copy or mutate
depending on sharing that its type does not mention. The question is whether a
checked discipline can make the in-place branch a property of a recognised
program fragment rather than of the current heap.

The obvious argument---``the variable is owned, so the count is one''---fails.
\lean{Inv} equates the stored count with the context's multiplicity, but does not
count owning roots: \lean{bindOwned} preserves \lean{Inv} while leaving the
multiplicity unchanged, and \lean{alias\_not\_accounted} exhibits the two-owner
state. The first repair is a representation: \lean{Accounted support H env c}
requires a duplicate-free, sound, complete and bound support, the live-cell
domain, and the equation \lean{rc = IncomingCount + EnvOwnerCount}. At count one
it yields \lean{count\_one\_dichotomy} and \lean{at\_most\_one\_owner}; at the
initializer it is established by \lean{Accounted.initCtx}
(\Cref{sec:accounting}).

The second obstacle is the emitted code. The take-out builds a pair whose fields
are the parent and the extracted child; the projections increment both; freeing
the unique pair releases both edges. The counts return to their entry values only
because the pair is freed: \lean{pair\_accounting} proves the boundary, and the
no-free control leaves a count of two. Restoration has two dispositions;
\lean{putBack\_exclusive\_of\_wellFormed} derives the mutate branch from the
protocol boundary and the child-call boundary, not from an assumed final state
(\Cref{sec:counted}).

The third obstacle is the compiler connection. The checker is about a model; the
paper relates the model to the operation trace the recogniser emits
(\lean{project\_sound}, \lean{recognized\_write\_in\_place}) and to the finite
nested path (\lean{index\_path\_simulates\_model}), with the child contract
derived at every level from one supplied leaf. The composed statements are three
results with different premises. \lean{principal\_write} composes the emitted,
model and runtime-model claims under the recogniser's verdict, the layout,
\lean{EntryInv}, \lean{Agrees}, the cell and bounds facts, and \lean{Corr}; it
takes \emph{no accounting premise}. Adjacent to it,
\lean{Prefix.toModel\_supported} places the projected flat model in
\lean{Supported}, so \lean{supported\_run} preserves \lean{Accounted} from an
accounted state given \lean{StoreSlots} for that model---a fact supplied at
\lean{execSet}, not generally for \lean{p.toModel}.
\lean{principal\_supported\_path} composes the finite-path correspondence, the
counted chain and the counted-prefix boundary from an accounted caller state
(\lean{hacc}) and conditional on \lean{CountedChildCall} (\lean{hcall}); the
emitted recursive callee is not captured, and the paper says so wherever the
claim appears (\Cref{sec:principal}).

The take-away is a chain of three proved relations---complete accounting, a
counted protocol at its accounted boundaries, and a fragment correspondence---not
a type system for Lean. The boundary is part of the result: C semantics, replay
and address identity, the emitted callee, trace-level agreement beyond the counted
prefix, extraction/restoration event coverage, whole-path non-growth, and the
source correspondence remain open (\Cref{sec:limits}).

\paragraph{Three contributions.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em, itemsep=2pt]\raggedright
\item \textbf{Complete accounting and preservation for the supported flat
fragment.} \lean{Accounted} with its support, domain and count fields,
\lean{Supported}, \lean{supported\_run} (from \lean{Accounted} plus the supplied
\lean{StoreSlots}), \lean{supported\_store\_exclusive}, \lean{storeSlots\_init},
and the independence and negative results \lean{alias\_not\_accounted},
\lean{aliasEntryInv} and \lean{accountedNotEntryInv}.
\item \textbf{A counted extraction, call and restoration protocol at its
accounted boundaries.} \lean{pair\_accounting},
\lean{pair\_traffic\_neutral}, \lean{no\_free\_components\_not\_exclusive},
\lean{counted\_takeOut}, \lean{counted\_restore}, \lean{counted\_nested\_chain},
\lean{putBack\_exclusive\_of\_wellFormed}, \lean{HeapCompat}, and the pair-live
refusal \lean{pairPhase\_refused}.
\item \textbf{Fragment correspondence and a composed guarantee with a closed
instance.} \lean{project\_sound}, \lean{recognized\_write\_in\_place},
\lean{index\_path\_simulates\_model}, \lean{childContracts\_of\_path},
\lean{principal\_write}, \lean{principal\_supported\_path} and
\lean{principal\_instance}, with the \lean{CountedChildCall}/N10 boundary
explicit.
\end{itemize}

The discipline is a deep embedding: \lean{Code} and \lean{NCode} are explicit
syntax interpreted by \lean{run}/\lean{runN}, and \lean{Ctx}, \lean{Heap},
\lean{check} and the theorems are ordinary Lean definitions. The source is Lean,
lowered to LCNF; a recognised prefix is mapped to a model program. The guarantee
is conditional on \lean{EntryInv}, \lean{Agrees}, the contracts A1--A12/N1--N12
and \lean{Corr}; it does not extend to the linked C runtime beyond the measured
gate.

\section{Setting and the running example}\label{sec:setting}
Lean~4 compiles through a sequence of IRs. The last LCNF phase inserts
reference-count operations, and the backend emits C. The runtime decides an array
update with \lean{lean\_is\_exclusive}: count one and non-persistent means in
place; every other case copies. In this setting the object is single-threaded;
count one and non-persistence characterise this test and are not a claim about
every Lean runtime mode. The development pins the toolchain, the sibling
revision and the captured LCNF artifacts, so every claim names the code it is
about.

The problem is not to implement reference counting, but to know statically that
the count at a particular write is one. Lean's source type system has no
linearity or uniqueness; adding one would be a language change. We therefore
state the property over the compiler's code and prove the connection, rather
than extend the type system.

\subsection{Model programs}
The checker does not run on Lean source or on LCNF directly. Its programs are
two small inductive types, interpreted over the model heap and environment; the
compiler connection is a mapping from the recognised LCNF to these values.

A \lean{LetF} is one of \lean{lit n}, \lean{size a}, \lean{isShared x} or
\lean{checkAlias x}.

\begin{minted}{lean4}
inductive Code where
  | let (x : Var) (v : LetF) (k : Code)
  | inc (x : Var) (n : Nat) (k : Code)
  | dec (x : Var) (n : Nat) (k : Code)
  | check (x : Var) (k : Code)
  | setArr (x : Var) (i : Var) (v : Var) (k : Code)
  | ret (x : Var)

inductive NCode where
  | base (c : Code)
  | takeOut (a i fst snd : Var) (e : Ref) (k : NCode)
  | putBack (a e i result : Var) (k : NCode)
  | callAt (state path x result : Var) (body : NCode) (k : NCode)
  | ret (x : Var)
\end{minted}

A \lean{Code} is the straight-line array-write fragment; an \lean{NCode} adds
extraction, restoration and the recursive call. Variables are \lean{Nat},
references are \lean{Nat}, \lean{Val} is a reference, a scalar or erased, and a
\lean{Heap} maps references to objects with a field array, a count and a
persistence flag. \lean{run}/\lean{runN} execute these values and
\lean{check}/\lean{checkN} are the checkers; the soundness theorems are about
this semantics. They are neither Lean source nor LCNF: \lean{projectPrefix} and
\lean{projectNested} walk an LCNF value, \lean{Prefix.toModel} and
\lean{indexModelTerm} read a \lean{Code}/\lean{NCode} off the recognised
operands, and \lean{execAST}/\lean{execNestedAST} execute the recognised trace.
The correspondence theorems relate that execution to the model run.

\subsection{The nested write}
The source is the array case of \lean{Layout5.setAt} in the sibling. It takes the
element out, checks the extracted focus, recurses on it and puts the result back:
\begin{minted}{lean4}
| _, .index i q, y, s =>
    if h : i < s.size then
      match takeOutAt (assertExclusiveArray openSite s) i h (stateDefault _) with
      | ⟨(sib, focus), hbound⟩ =>
        plugAt sib i hbound (setAt q y (assertExclusive structSite focus))
    else s
\end{minted}
The emitted C for this arm is the sequence the development names: a
\lean{lean\_is\_exclusive} test for the array, the \lean{takeOut} exchange
(\lean{lean\_array\_fget}, \lean{lean\_array\_fset}, the pair allocation), the
child check on the extracted element, the recursive call and the
\lean{lean\_array\_fset} put-back. The recogniser \lean{projectNested} accepts
the \lean{Path.index} arm, \lean{projectPair} accepts the two pair arms, and
\lean{execNestedAST} executes the accepted trace. The model term is
\lean{Path.build path value body ls}; \lean{checkN} checks it from
\lean{initCtx}.

The example separates what the tool computes from what a client supplies. The
compiler computes the LCNF and the C; \lean{projectNested} computes the prefix;
\lean{Prefix.toModel}/\lean{indexModelTerm} compute the model program;
\lean{checkN} computes the checker result. The client supplies the source
declaration, the \lean{Path} and the take-out name, and the proofs of
\lean{Agrees}, \lean{LevelMatch}, \lean{PathContract} and \lean{EntryInv} (the
path/slot and entry facts the checker cannot see). The recursive call is a
contract on an admitted model body: the child contract is derived at every level
by the finite-path derivation, with one leaf computation supplied. The depth-two
witness instance in \lean{OwnershipTest/PathRecursion.lean} takes model index 1
at both levels and leaf \lean{.base (.setArr 9 5 2 (.ret 9))}; its tip slot 1
goes from scalar 6 to scalar 1, the root and child slot 1 are restored to
references 1 and 3, and the root's sibling slot 0 keeps scalar 5. A deeper level
that takes its index by
re-projecting the continuation is outside the model, because the flat
environment cannot express the emitted per-invocation index; the emitted call's
meaning (N10) is not captured. \lean{OwnershipTest/PathConsolidation.lean}
supplies a depth-three leaf in \lean{takeOut}/\lean{putBack} form as a
supplied-leaf witness, not an instance of the correspondence.

\section{The accounting invariant}\label{sec:accounting}
The model heap, its counts and exclusivity are the semantic side of the
judgement: \lean{Inv} ties the context's multiplicity to the heap count, and
\lean{Exclusive} is count one and non-persistence.

\needspace{10\baselineskip}\begin{claim}[The raw invariant and the exclusivity predicate]\label{def:inv}\end{claim}
\begin{minted}{lean4}
structure Inv (H : Heap) (env : Env) (c : Ctx) : Prop where
  rc_eq         : ∀ r, H.rc r = c.mult r
  owned         : ∀ x r, c.us x = .owned → c.refOf x = some r →
                    env x = some (.ref r) ∧ 1 ≤ c.mult r
  borrow        : ∀ x r, c.us x = .borrow → c.refOf x = some r →
                    env x = some (.ref r) ∧ H.cells r ≠ none
  scalar        : ∀ x, c.us x = .scalar → ∃ n, env x = some (.scalar n)
  nonPersistent : ∀ r o, H.cells r = some o → o.persistent = false
\end{minted}
\noindent\lean{Exclusive H r} means that \lean{r} is a live, non-persistent cell
whose count is one.

The displayed invariant relates the heap count to the context multiplicity, but
it does not count the environment's owning variables: \lean{Inv.owned} only
requires \lean{1 ≤ c.mult r}. \lean{bindOwned} preserves \lean{Inv} without
changing \lean{mult}, so two owned variables can denote one count-one cell. The
raw invariant therefore does not imply physical uniqueness. The kernel
countermodel \lean{alias\_not\_accounted} constructs exactly that state:
\lean{initCtx} has one owner, and binding a second owned variable leaves the
multiplicity at one. The initializer's context does satisfy
\lean{OwnersAccounted} (\lean{initCtx\_accounted}). \lean{CountFidelity} is not a
consequence of \lean{Inv}, but it is a consequence of the relation below
(\lean{countFidelity}); the residual runtime fidelity is not proved.

\needspace{10\baselineskip}\begin{claim}[Complete accounting]\label{def:accounted}\end{claim}
\begin{minted}{lean4}
structure Accounted (support : List Var) (H : Heap) (env : Env) (c : Ctx) : Prop where
  inv              : Inv H env c
  support_nodup    : support.Nodup
  support_sound    : ∀ x, x ∈ support → c.us x = .owned
  support_complete : ∀ x, c.us x = .owned → x ∈ support
  support_bound    : ∀ x, x ∈ support → ∃ r, c.refOf x = some r
  domain           : ∀ r o, H.cells r = some o → r < H.next
  count            : ∀ r, H.rc r = IncomingCount H r + EnvOwnerCount c support r
\end{minted}

The relation is deliberately redundant: the support is a duplicate-free
enumeration of every owning variable, each entry is bound to a reference, every
live cell lies inside the range the edge count enumerates, and the count
equation holds over two independently computed quantities.

\begin{minted}{lean4}
def IncomingCount (H : Heap) (r : Ref) : Nat :=
  ((List.range H.next).map fun q =>
    match H.cells q with
    | some o => (o.fields.toList.filter (fun v => v = .ref r)).length
    | none => 0).sum

def EnvOwnerCount (c : Ctx) (vars : List Var) (r : Ref) : Nat :=
  (vars.filter (fun x => c.us x = .owned ∧ c.refOf x = some r)).length

def CountFidelity (H : Heap) (c : Ctx) (vars : List Var) : Prop :=
  ∀ r, H.rc r = IncomingCount H r + EnvOwnerCount c vars r
\end{minted}

\lean{mult\_eq} reads the equation through \lean{Inv.rc\_eq};
\lean{count\_one\_dichotomy} splits count one into one owning root with no
incoming edge, or no owner with exactly one incoming edge;
\lean{at\_most\_one\_owner} says two owning variables at count one are the same
variable; \lean{countFidelity} is the equation in the retained predicate's shape;
and \lean{ownersAccounted} recovers the environment-side predicate. The
initializer establishes the relation (\lean{initCtx}).

\paragraph{The supported fragment.}
\lean{Supported c s} is indexed at the pre-state \lean{c}; each constructor
states the checker's own rule at \lean{c} and its continuation at the checker's
post-state, and carries the certificates the checker cannot see: a fresh alias
target and a fresh scalar-\lean{.let} target. The fragment has eight
constructors; \lean{.inc} is absent and every \lean{.dec} is the single-count
form \lean{.dec x 1}, where \lean{decOne}'s premise is exactly the checker's
\lean{n < c.mult r} at \lean{n = 1}. From \lean{Supported c s} the checker
accepts the program (\lean{supported\_check}), which is what lets a preservation
theorem follow a fault-free run. The checker itself is unchanged.

The store's slot fact is deliberately absent from the constructors: no context
records which value a cell's selected slot holds, so it is the \lean{StoreSlots}
premise of \lean{supported\_run} rather than a constructor premise.
\lean{StoreSlots H env s} is a recursive predicate over the supported program,
supplied from the heap and environment alone (it may not assume the invariant):
at the store the run reaches, the array cell's selected slot holds a scalar, so
the in-place write replaces a value rather than an edge. \lean{storeSlots\_init}
discharges it for the initializer's measured write; it is not a general
\lean{StoreSlots} for \lean{p.toModel}.

\section{The checker and preservation}\label{sec:checker}
\subsection{The judgement and its rules}
A context records, for each variable, whether it is owned, borrowed, scalar or
dead, which reference it denotes, and the multiplicity of that reference. The
checker is a total function from a context and a program to a successor context
or failure; the frame stack records open extractions. The rules in
Table~\ref{tab:rules} are ownership obligations: a write is admitted only for an
owned handle of multiplicity one, an extraction only with the frame discipline,
and a call only with an isolated body that returns the state it was handed.

The object language is untyped: there is no type former for a program, and the
checker does not infer one by unification. The typing judgement is
$c \vdash \mathit{code} \Rightarrow c'$, defined to hold exactly when
\lean{check c code = some c'}; the context is the type of the program point. A
context is a usage, reference and multiplicity map; the checker is
syntax-directed.

\begin{minted}{lean4}
inductive U where
  | owned
  | borrow
  | scalar
  | dead

structure Ctx where
  us    : Var → U
  refOf : Var → Option Ref
  mult  : Ref → Nat
\end{minted}
\noindent A frame records the array, index and element of an open extraction;
\lean{check : Ctx → Code → Option Ctx} and
\lean{checkNF : Ctx → List Frame → NCode → Option Ctx} are total.

The judgement is syntax-directed: each constructor has one rule, the context is
an input, and the successor context is computed by \lean{setUs}, \lean{move},
\lean{bindOwned}, \lean{inc} and \lean{dec}. There are no metavariables, no
unification and no implicit type arguments in the object language; the only
computation is the context update. This is why the discipline makes the count
visible at the type level: at the write, the array's handle has usage
\lean{owned} and multiplicity one in the context, and the rule refuses every
other usage.

Two consequences are worth stating. \lean{dec} is affine, not the last drop: its
premise is $n <$ the object's multiplicity, so it consumes a duplicate handle and
leaves the object live. And linearity at the write is the side condition
\lean{multOf x = 1}, not the usage tag alone.

\paragraph{A derivation.}
The first example is the two rules applied in sequence. The premise of the
second rule is the context the first produces; no type variable is solved.
\[
\frac{
  \begin{array}{c}
  \text{us } 0 = \text{owned} \qquad \text{multOf } 0 = 1 \\
  c_1 = c_0.\text{move } 0\ 3
  \end{array}
}{
  c_0 \vdash \texttt{checkAlias } 0 \Rightarrow c_1
}
\qquad
\frac{
  \begin{array}{c}
  \text{us } 3 = \text{owned} \qquad \text{multOf } 3 = 1 \\
  \text{us } 1 = \text{us } 2 = \text{scalar} \qquad
  c_1 \vdash \texttt{ret } 3 \Rightarrow c_1
  \end{array}
}{
  c_1 \vdash \texttt{setArr } 3\ 1\ 2;\ \texttt{ret } 3 \Rightarrow c_1
}
\]
Composing the two gives
$c_0 \vdash \texttt{execSet } 0\ 3\ 1\ 2 \Rightarrow c_1$, i.e.
\lean{check (initCtx 0) (execSet 0 3 1 2) = some ((initCtx 0).move 0 3)}.
The checker computes $c_1$; the derivation is the rule applications.

The frame stack is the local form of the ownership invariant. An extraction
opens a frame naming the array, index and element; a put-back closes the matching
frame; a call checks its body under an isolated context and continues with the
result.

\subsection{Two examples}
\needspace{10\baselineskip}\begin{example}[A unique write]\label{ex:unique}\end{example}
\begin{minted}{lean4}
def execSet (x y i v : Var) : Code :=
  .let y (.checkAlias x) (.setArr y i v (.ret y))

#guard (check (initCtx 0) (execSet 0 3 1 2)).isSome
\end{minted}

\needspace{10\baselineskip}\begin{example}[An aliased write is refused]\label{ex:alias}\end{example}
\begin{minted}{lean4}
def aliasSet (x y i v : Var) : Code :=
  .inc x 1 (execSet x y i v)

#guard (check (initCtx 0) (aliasSet 0 3 1 2)).isNone
\end{minted}
The alias increments the handle, so the write's multiplicity-one obligation
fails; the same refusal appears at the context level for a heap whose count is
two. This is the static counterpart of the runtime copy: the checker rejects the
program before it can copy.

\subsection{The invariant and soundness}
The entry invariant adds the facts a local checker cannot see: the invariant
tree, client separation and root completeness. For fresh arrays
\lean{initEntryInv} establishes it, and \lean{checkN\_sound\_of\_entryInv}
combines it with the checker.

\lean{check\_sound} says that an accepted program run from an invariant state
finishes in a state that satisfies the invariant and records only exclusive
writes. \lean{EntryInv} extends \lean{Inv} with the invariant tree, client
separation and root completeness, and \lean{checkN\_sound\_of\_entryInv} adds
those facts and \lean{Agrees} to the checker's acceptance, concluding the same
invariant and fault-free run for the nested program.

The checker's local rules do not name the heap's path structure; the path theory
supplies it, composing agreement through take-out, call and put-back and lifting
the checker to every finite path. The extraction and restoration stores are not trace
events; their target-cell dispositions are the model facts
\lean{checkN\_extraction\_exclusive} and
\lean{checkN\_extraction\_exclusive\_of\_entryInv}.

\subsection{Preservation through the supported fragment}
\lean{Supported} is the counted part of the checker's accepted set. A supported
program from an accounted state, with the store-slot premise, runs without a
fault, records only exclusive writes, satisfies the complete relation at every
state it reaches, and satisfies it at the final state at the support the step
arithmetic determines.

\needspace{10\baselineskip}\begin{claim}[Supported execution preserves accounting]\label{thm:supported-run}\end{claim}
\begin{minted}{lean4}
theorem supported_run {c : Ctx} {s : Code} (hs : Supported c s)
    {support : List Var} {H : Heap} {env : Env}
    (hacc : Accounted support H env c) (hslots : StoreSlots H env s) :
    ∃ c' H' env' v ws,
      check c s = some c' ∧
      run s env H = .done H' env' v ws ∧
      (∀ w ∈ ws, w.2 = true) ∧
      Accounted (resultSupport s support) H' env' c' ∧
      AccountedAlong H env c support s
\end{minted}

\lean{AccountedAlong} is the conclusion, never a hypothesis: at each instruction
the relation holds at the interpreter's own post-state, and at each array store
the target is exclusive. \lean{supported\_store\_exclusive} states the store's
own pre- and post-state: it is reached with its target exclusive and its slot
scalar, and the post-state is accounted at the checker's unchanged context.
\lean{storeSlots\_init} supplies the slot for the initializer's measured write.

\paragraph{Three hard cases.}
The projections temporarily raise both counts because the pair's edge and the
projected handle are both live;
the free of the unique pair removes exactly those two edges, which is why
\lean{pair\_accounting} returns to the entry counts and the no-free control does
not. The suspended parent remains exclusive across the child call because the
child's contract is confined to the child's mutable footprint, not because the
parent's exclusivity is assumed: \lean{callAt\_frame\_confined} takes
\lean{AnnotationsIn}, checker acceptance, the return variable, the isolated
\lean{Inv} and \lean{TakeOutsOK}; the parent/child separation is a
\lean{ChildCall.separated} fact used in the path derivation. And the put-back's
mutate branch is derived from
the protocol boundary and the child-call boundary, not from an assumed final
state.

\section{Counted protocol and fragment correspondence}\label{sec:counted}
\subsection{Recognition and the flat correspondence}
A source-level discipline is only useful if it survives lowering. The compiler
rewrites the program, inlines helpers and chooses the emitted shape; the
recogniser walks the actual LCNF value, pinning operation order, arities,
operand identities and branch constructors. It rejects the token-preserving near
misses (eleven at the top level, eleven on the nested path, fourteen per pair
arm), so the correspondence is about the code we name. No universal rejection
theorem is claimed. The accepted grammar fixes the check's argument, the
array-set operands and their order, the pair constructor and projections, the
two \lean{Path} branches and their join, and the continuation boundary.

\lean{project\_sound} gives executed/model equality for the abstract prefix;
\lean{EntryInv} and layout yield \lean{recognized\_write\_in\_place}, concluding
both give \lean{inPlaceOutcome root i v o H}.

\lean{PrefixOutcome} records the target, result/\lean{setResult} binders, heap,
disposition, fault and write trace, discarding the variable map and suffix; its
trace preserves each write's exclusive/copy bit, and the flat prefix has the
single \lean{(root, true)} event.
\lean{execAST\_recognized} is decoding adequacy, not a semantics; A1--A12 are
the independent meanings, which is why decoding is load-bearing.

\subsection{The finite nested path}
The model nests take-out, call and put-back per level; the finite-path induction
discharges the callee contract from the tail and leaves only the leaf contract
and the emitted call's meaning (N10). The flat environment cannot express the
emitted per-invocation \lean{oproj[1]} index; that bound is recorded.

\lean{index\_path\_simulates\_model} concludes that the executed nested prefix's
\lean{NestedOutcome} is the model run of
\lean{Path.build path value body (l :: rest)}, completing with an all-exclusive
trace, under the recogniser's verdict, layout, level match, agreement and path
contract.

For a finite level path the contract is derived at every level:
\lean{childContracts\_of\_path} takes the level list and one leaf computation and
yields the child contract at each level, whose body is the remaining levels'
\lean{build}; \lean{childContract\_of\_path} is the single-step form. The leaf
computation (agreement, checker acceptance, exit variable, count-freedom,
annotation) is the one supplied function-level fact. The claim is the finite
path's syntax, not data-dependent recursion. The emitted callee is not captured:
\lean{execNested}'s call rule runs the admitted model body with only the call's
state, path and value (N10); a deeper level that re-indexes by re-projecting the
continuation is outside the model.

\subsection{Counted extraction, call and restoration}
The take-out builds a pair of the parent and extracted child; the constructor
moves the handles in, the projections increment both, and freeing the unique pair
releases both edges. The counts return to their entry values only because the
pair is freed: the negative controls show that omitting the free, or retaining an
alias, leaves a count of two.

\needspace{10\baselineskip}\begin{claim}[Pair accounting]\label{thm:pair}\end{claim}
\begin{minted}{lean4}
theorem pair_accounting {H : Heap} {p c : Ref} {i : Nat}
    (hw : WellFormedEntry H p c i) :
    let B := protocol H p c i
    B.2.rc p = 1 ∧ B.2.rc c = 1 ∧ B.2.cells p ≠ none ∧
      B.2.cells c ≠ none ∧ B.2.cells B.1 = none ∧
      Heap.Exclusive B.2 p ∧ Heap.Exclusive B.2 c ∧
      B.2.rc p = H.rc p ∧ B.2.rc c = H.rc c
\end{minted}
The no-free control, \lean{no\_free\_components\_not\_exclusive}, leaves both
components at count two, neither exclusive, and the pair live;
\lean{WellFormedEntry} fixes both at count one.

\subsection{The runtime model}
\lean{OwnershipLcnf.RuntimeModel} is a second operational model, related to
\lean{Ownership.Heap} by \lean{Corr}: the correspondence is between two models and
binds no physical C object or address. It preserves refs, cells, counts,
persistence, fields and cursor, so model exclusivity implies the check returns and
the write mutates.

Three statements carry the reading: \lean{checkExclusive\_no\_abort} (under
\lean{Corr} and model exclusivity the check never returns \lean{abort}),
\lean{setAt\_inPlace\_of\_entryInv} (the set returns in place, preserves
\lean{Corr}, keeps the target and writes the scalar), and
\lean{prefix\_abort\_impossible} (the same at the emitted-semantics boundary).
None binds a physical C object or address.

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}
Huisinga's 2023 thesis \cite{huisinga} is the closest predecessor. It analyses a
model of Lean's IR rather than Lean source or emitted LCNF, enforces uniqueness
through a static analysis, and studies the analysis's checker; uniqueness is a
static property of the analysis, not the runtime count; there is no compiler
link; and soundness and compiler integration remain future work there. This work
differs in what is connected: it proves a count-one checker against a model heap
and composes that result with the recognised emitted prefix and a runtime model
at the flat write, while the counted protocol is isolated at its accounted
boundaries. It is not a claim to the first static uniqueness analysis for Lean.

Perceus \cite{perceus} and Counting Immutable Beans \cite{countingbeans} turn
ownership into a compilation discipline: an ownership pass inserts counts and
reuse, the runtime maintains them, and the correctness argument is for the
transformation. This work shares the count-one target but asks a different
question: a checker over a model, with a proved relation between owning roots
and incoming heap edges, boundary accounting for the emitted extraction pair, and
an exclusive restoration derived from the protocol boundary. Perceus optimises
allocation and reuse; \lean{Accounted} specifies when count one is justified.

Linear Haskell \cite{linearhaskell} makes linearity a type-system property
through linear arrows and multiplicities; Clean's uniqueness typing
\cite{uniqueness} is uniqueness, not linearity. Neither is a count-one checker
over an existing compiler IR. Lean's \lean{Array.markLinear} \cite{marklinear} is
dynamic: it can copy an array to establish uniqueness and, with
\lean{LEAN\_ABORT\_ON\_NONLINEAR}, abort on later nonlinear use. Translation
validation \cite{translationvalidation} checks a particular compiler run, not a
static discipline. Verified compilation \cite{compcert,cakeml} proves a compiler
correct for its source language; it is a different category, and we make no
comparison row for it.

\section{Limits and conclusion}\label{sec:limits}
\begin{nonclaim}
The C semantics of the primitives, replay-to-backend identity (A10), address
identity, free and cascade, and the source correspondence are not formalised or
attempted; the emitted recursive callee and continuation (N10) are not captured;
the flat environment cannot express the per-invocation index; the leaf body and
several heap-shape facts are supplied; persistence, concurrency and callbacks are
outside the fragment; and the nested linked runtime is not measured.

The accounting layer is not a consequence of \lean{Inv}: \lean{OwnersAccounted}
has a kernel countermodel and explicit \lean{bindOwned}/\lean{dec} preconditions,
and the checker's certificates are carried by \lean{Supported}.
\lean{CountFidelity} is the count equation carried by \lean{Accounted}, but the
residual runtime fidelity is not proved. \lean{StoreSlots} is supplied, and
\lean{storeSlots\_init} covers only the initializer's measured write; the fragment
excludes \lean{.inc} and admits only \lean{.dec x 1}.

The composed statements do not transport accounting. \lean{principal\_write}
takes no accounting premise and does not conclude one;
\lean{principal\_accounted} consumes \lean{Accounted} at the initializer.
\lean{principal\_supported\_path} is conditional on the caller's complete
accounting (\lean{hacc}) and the child-call contract (\lean{hcall}); its child
call is an admitted model body, not the emitted callee, and its conclusion is the
reached in-place store at one level, not whole-path non-growth outside the root's
mutable footprint. Beyond the counted prefix no trace-level agreement is claimed.
The nested trace omits extraction and restoration stores, but each target cell is
proved exclusive by \lean{checkN\_extraction\_exclusive\_of\_entryInv}, a separate
fact rather than a silent absence. The pair-arms correspondence with
\lean{Ownership.Pair.runP} is a named next step.

The recognition claim is scoped to the accepted grammar and the mutation corpus,
not a universal rejection theorem. The differential mode compares recorded
expectations rather than executing the \lean{Ownership} model. The probe returns
two on an ordinary mismatch,
but \lean{sealed} returns one and so cannot be distinguished from the exclusivity
abort; that path is not exercised. The table's observations are at
\lean{d330fa0}, two commits behind the Lean development's head, not a current
re-run.
\end{nonclaim}

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
The count-one discipline is proved at the model, the recognised LCNF and the
runtime model for the flat write, composed as \lean{principal\_write}, which takes
no accounting premise. The nested \lean{principal\_supported\_path} composes the
finite-path correspondence, the counted chain and the counted-prefix boundary at
one level, from an accounted caller state (\lean{hacc}) and conditional on the
child-call contract (\lean{hcall}); beyond that boundary, and the emitted callee,
remain open. The result buys a checked count one for a recognised fragment, not a
type system or a physical count. Next: the C semantics, replay/backend and
address identity, free and cascade, and the emitted callee; soundness is proved,
not completeness.
